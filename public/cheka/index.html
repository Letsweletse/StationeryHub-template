Artist's impression

Two hours from Gaborone, a world away from it.

Cheka Kalahari Domes is a small glamping retreat taking shape beside the A2, the Trans-Kalahari Highway, just beyond Jwaneng. Each dome sits raised on its own deck, facing west into the sunset, with real beds, air conditioning, a private bathroom and nothing between you and the sky.

It is built for the people who already travel this road: families on the way to Ghanzi and Namibia, teams working in Jwaneng, and couples from Gaborone who need one quiet night away.

Everything a road-weary traveller needs

Safe, warm and comfortable, with the wild Kalahari right outside the fence.

Floodlit at night

Tall floodlights around the fence and parking, warm path lights to every dome.

Gated & guarded

One gate in and out, a guard house, and a log-pole fence around the whole site.

Hot water, always

A wood-fired donkey boiler and solar geysers for proper hot showers, even in July.

Big JoJo tanks

Large JoJo tanks on a raised stand, plus a private tank at every dome for showers.

Plenty of parking

Space for cars, bakkies, trailers and overland rigs, inside the secured fence.

Bar deck

A thatched deck bar for sundowners, cold drinks and coffee, facing the sunset.

Braai boma

A communal fire pit with log seating, braai packs and firewood from the kitchen.

Solar powered

Solar panels and batteries keep lights, fridges and air conditioning running.

One evening at the domes

The best part of the Kalahari happens between five in the afternoon and six the next morning. This is how we want your stay to feel.

16:30

Turn off the A2

A short sand track, secure parking, and a cold welcome drink before you have even unpacked.

18:15

Sunset from your deck

Every dome faces west. The sky goes gold, then copper, then violet over the acacias.

19:30

Fire and braai

Your own fire pit, a braai pack from the kitchen, and the quiet only the desert can make.

22:00

The Milky Way

No city glow for miles. Lie back on the deck, or watch the stars through the dome's clear panel.

06:00

Coffee at sunrise

Birdsong, cool air, and the day's first light on the sand before you hit the road again.

Inside the dome

A proper room, not a tent

  • Queen bed facing the glass so you wake up to the Kalahari sunrise.
  • Skylight above the bed for the stars, with a blackout cover for sleep.
  • Rattan hanging chair and velvet sofa by the panoramic window.
  • Air conditioning and insulation for hot summers and cold July nights.
  • Woven pendant light and warm lamps instead of harsh bulbs.
  • Wooden floor, rugs and floor cushions, with blackout curtains all round.
  • Private bathroom pod with a hot shower, basin and WC.

Cheka Kalahari Domes

The photo spot

Your first photo starts at the gate.

Our name is carved into a heavy wooden plank across two log posts, with lanterns that glow at dusk. It's built for the picture every guest takes on arrival, and the one they post before they leave.

Who we are building it for

A stop on the road. A reason to come back.

The Trans-Kalahari carries travellers between Gauteng, Gaborone and Walvis Bay every day. We want the best night of that journey to be here.

On the road

Overland travellers

Self-drive families and tourists heading to Kang, Ghanzi, the Central Kalahari or Namibia. Arrive late, leave early, sleep well.

In Jwaneng for work

Business stays

Contractors, consultants and visiting teams. Stay near Jwaneng and sleep in the Kalahari, with Wi-Fi and invoicing for companies.

From Gaborone

Weekend escapes

Couples, birthdays and small groups. Two hours of driving for a sunset, a fire and a sky full of stars.

Getting here

Follow the A2 west from Gaborone through Kanye and Jwaneng. The retreat sits just beyond Jwaneng, on the road towards Sekoma and Kang.
